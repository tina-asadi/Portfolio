Case study

SEO Report

A period-over-period SEO and audience report for a large Persian-language health and beauty blog, built so each page answers one question: what grew, what dropped, and where to look next.

Role
SEO analysis and reporting
Collaborator

Tools
Google Search Console, Google Analytics
Focus
Organic growth, reporting
The brief The approach Designing the report What the data showed The results

01

The brief

The site is a large Persian-language blog about health, skin, hair and mental wellbeing, and almost all of its visitors arrive from Google. The team needed a report they could read in a few minutes: how organic search was performing, which pages and searches were driving it, and what had changed since the previous period.

Raw Search Console and Analytics screens show everything and explain nothing. My goal was a report where each page answers one question, every number is compared with the previous period, and the changes that matter are obvious at a glance.

Compare, don’t just count

A figure on its own says little. Every metric sits beside the previous period.

One question per page

Clicks, audience, channels, winners and losers each get their own page.

Direction before detail

Green and red change values show what moved before anyone reads a number.

02

The approach

I worked in ten-day windows and compared each one with the window before it, so movement was visible quickly instead of waiting for a monthly summary.

  1. 1. CollectSearch Console for queries, pages, clicks and impressions. Analytics for users, channels and audience.
  2. 2. CompareEvery metric against the previous period
  3. 3. SegmentBy channel, device, city and page type
  4. 4. Find moversThe queries and pages that gained or lost the most clicks
  5. 5. DesignOne question per page, in a consistent layout
  6. 6. CheckVerify every number against the source before sending

03

Designing the report

The report is a set of single-purpose pages. A KPI strip comes first so the headline is read in seconds. Trend charts overlay the current period on the previous one, tables hold the exact figures, and a consistent two-color scheme means a reader never has to relearn the chart. Move your cursor over the charts below to tilt the report.

KPI strip first

Clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position, each with its change.

Current vs previous

Two lines on one chart make an improvement or a dip obvious without a legend lesson.

Color carries meaning

Green for up, red for down, and a muted tone for no change.

04

What the data showed

Each finding below came straight from a page of the report.

Search is the engine

Organic search brought about 90% of the 323,930 users in the period: 291,521 users, up 6%.

Phones come first

94.2% of users were on mobile, so the mobile experience matters far more than desktop here.

The blog is the product

The blog earned 767,357 page views, about 95% of all page views, and grew 7.63%.

One article took off

A mental-health article went from 2,739 to 14,203 clicks, a gain of 11,464 and the biggest jump of any page.

Drops need attention too

The seasonal-allergy and micellar-water articles lost 695 and 663 clicks, which makes them candidates for a content refresh.

Top searches rank near the top

The ten biggest queries all average positions between 1.03 and 2.6, with click-through rates from 12.95% to 67.6%.

05

The results

These numbers describe how the whole site performed in the periods shown. They come from a team working on a large site, so I don’t claim them as mine alone, but they are the results the report was built to track.

17.9MSearch clicks over 16 months
264MSearch impressions over 16 months
36%More views on Google Search, 2.86M in the period shown
34%More clicks, 1.46M in the same period
387KClicks in the latest ten-day window, up 8.9%
  • Comparing every metric with the previous period made changes visible. A number on its own rarely tells a story.
  • Pages that fell deserve as much attention as pages that grew. The reduction tables point to content worth refreshing.
  • Next time I will check every percentage against the raw numbers before the report goes out, and show gaps in tracking as follow-up tasks.
  • Next: put a recommendation beside each finding, so the report ends with actions and not only observations.
