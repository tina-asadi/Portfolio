Watch Store

A smart watch store that started as a Figma design and became a working storefront, with a cart, a wishlist, live search and a quick view. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

01

The plan

I designed a smart watch store in Figma, and I wanted to find out what happens to a design when it has to work. Does the carousel still feel good when it moves? Does the cart make sense when the numbers change? A design only answers those questions once it is built.

So the plan had two parts: finish the design properly, with wireframes, a UI kit and high-fidelity screens for desktop and phone, and then build every screen as a real storefront.

  • Keep the minimal, premium look of the design, with a dark hero and clean product cards.
  • Make shopping feel complete: add to cart, wishlist, search, quick view and a free-shipping nudge.
  • Make it accessible and fast, with large tap targets and light images.
  • Keep the code simple enough to edit: products live in one file.

02

The idea

A watch is a small, precise object, so the store should feel precise too. The design leads with one big watch on a black hero, then lets quiet white cards do the selling.

A hero you can steer

A carousel with a progress ring, arrows, keyboard control and swiping, that pauses when you hover or focus it.

Quick view instead of page hopping

Tap any product to see details, pick a band colour and add it to the cart without leaving the page.

A cart that guides

The drawer shows a free-shipping progress bar, so the next best action is always visible.

A wishlist

Heart any watch, see a badge in the header, and filter the products page by your saved watches.

Search from anywhere

Press / on any page to search the whole catalogue, with instant suggestions.

Smart Watch Studio

An interactive collage of watches: hover one to focus it and see its name and price, click to open it.

05

Building it

The store is four pages of plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no build step. To add or edit a product you change one file, and every page updates.

Product data in one place

Products, hero slides, studio layout and stores live in a single JavaScript file, and every page reads from it.

Cart and wishlist that remember

Both are saved in localStorage, so they survive a refresh. The add-to-cart button confirms with “Added ✓”.

Filters you can share

Category, search and sort on the products page are kept in the address, so any filtered view can be sent as a link.

Live open or closed

Each store shows whether it is open right now, with directions and tap-to-call.

Accessible by default

A skip link, visible focus, ARIA labels and live regions, Escape closes overlays, all tap targets are at least 44 pixels, and reduced motion is respected.

Light images

Every image is converted to WebP, trimmed and lazy-loaded, about 420 KB in total.

Bugs found and fixed

Building from the design exposed copy mistakes that I fixed: “Add to card” became “Add to cart”, “Supper” became “Super”, and “Servises” became “Services”.

The result

The Watch Store went from a Figma file to a store that works on every screen size. It is the clearest example in my portfolio of taking a design all the way to code.
